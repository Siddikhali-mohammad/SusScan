Methodology

Three models. One verdict.

Each model examines the image through a different architectural lens. A verdict requires at least two of the three models to agree — no single model can force a result.

🔬
EfficientNet-B4
CNN

Scans local pixel-level patterns — blending boundaries, texture discontinuities, and compression artifacts that reveal where a face was composited.

Detects: GAN artifacts, JPEG block patterns, frequency anomalies
Training weight: 0.39×
→
⚡
Swin Transformer
Vision Transformer

Uses hierarchical shifted-window attention to detect global structural inconsistencies — unnatural geometry, lighting mismatches, and spatial artifacts CNNs can miss.

Detects: geometric warping, lighting inconsistencies, structural irregularities
Training weight: 1.33×
→
🧠
CLIP ViT-B/16
Multimodal ViT

Pre-trained on 400M image-text pairs, it brings semantic understanding — detecting subtle realism gaps that signal AI generation even at high visual quality.

Detects: semantic unnaturalness, AI-generated textures, realism gaps
Training weight: 2.34×

Stacked Ensemble Meta-Learner

A logistic regression meta-learner combines all three model scores using leave-one-dataset-out cross-validation. A FAKE verdict requires the meta-learner score to exceed 62% and at least 2 of 3 base models to individually agree — preventing any single model from forcing a false verdict. Scores between 40–62% are returned as UNCERTAIN.

Training Data

Trained across 8 public deepfake datasets totalling 428K images: FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF v2, DFDC, real-and-fake-face, artifact dataset, SD-generated faces, adversarial examples, and a clean real-world subset. Leave-one-dataset-out cross-validation ensures the ensemble generalises beyond any single source.

What GradCAM Shows

After scoring, Gradient-weighted Class Activation Maps (GradCAM++) highlight which face regions most influenced the decision. The Activation Hotspot shows the top 30% of model attention, while Detected Regions maps those activations to named facial zones — forehead, eyes, jawline, edges — with plain-language explanations of what each signal means.

Know the limits

When to trust the result — and when not to.

No deepfake detector is perfect. Understanding where the model is reliable and where it isn't helps you interpret results correctly.

⚠️
Real images that may be flagged FAKE
False positives — model sees manipulation artifacts where there are none
📱 Portrait mode photos — face/background compositing produces edge artifacts identical to face-swap seams
✨ Beauty filters & skin smoothing — AI texture blending mimics GAN synthesis patterns
📤 Social media re-encoded images — repeated JPEG compression creates block artifacts similar to deepfake artifacts
🖼 Heavy photo editing / Photoshop — compositing, retouching and layer blending leave detectable seams
🌙 Extreme lighting conditions — very dark, overexposed, or neon-lit photos create unnatural texture responses
🔍
Fake images that may pass as REAL
False negatives — manipulation too subtle or too different from training data
🤖 High-quality commercial deepfakes — state-of-the-art models (e.g. HyperReenact, DiffSwap) produce near-zero artifacts
🖼 Fully AI-generated faces (GAN / Diffusion) — no real face underneath means no compositing seams; purely synthetic faces can fool the model
📉 Low resolution or heavily compressed fakes — compression masks manipulation artifacts, reducing detection signal
🎭 Adversarial deepfakes — images crafted specifically to fool detectors by adding imperceptible noise patterns
↩ Deepfakes post-processed to look old / degraded — film grain, scan artifacts and noise can wash out detection signals
